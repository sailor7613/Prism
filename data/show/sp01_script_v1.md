## 2 · The long 2014
**TED:** Hello and welcome to the Getty Villa. I'm Ted Turner, and this is a Parallelogram Report special.
No bill tonight. No vote coming up. A movie. Two movies, actually.
My friend Nate — Nathan, y2kmindset on Twitter — has a name for the decade we've been living in. He calls it the long 2014.
Here's what he means. Around 2014, the feeds learned something. The fastest way to get your attention is to hand you a side.
So every new thing that comes out gets run through two camps. You're for it or you're against it. Either way, it wins.
The calendar kept moving. The culture didn't. That's the long 2014. Everything new gets sorted into the same two piles.

## 3 · Cannot go anywhere

Megyn Kelly saw You Can See Everything — the Nathan Fielder documentary about Elizabeth Holmes's last weeks before prison — the weekend it opened. Here's her review.
**[TAPE — 0:06–0:46]** "Go see it… worth your time… it's fun to be surprised…"

**[TAPE — 2:21–3:30]**

## 4 · 1992
**TED:** So why does one scene beat three hours?
In 1992 — the year I was born — Francis Fukuyama sat down with Charlie Rose to talk about The End of History and the Last Man. Rose read him the critics: with no rival left, we're headed for a world of banality. Here's the man who said history was over.
**[TAPE — 1992, 502.8–530.0]** "…if people say that, I agree with them perfectly… there's a side of man that really wants great causes… struggle and sacrifice… risk and daring."
**TED:** I agree with them perfectly. There's a side of us that wants great causes. Peace and prosperity don't feed it. He had a Greek word for that side.
**[TAPE — 1992, 283–301]** "…the part of the personality that demands recognition from other people…"
**TED:** Thymos. The part of you that wants to be recognized. Not fed. Not paid. Seen.
His worry was that once the big fight ended, that hunger would have nowhere to go. In the 1989 essay that started it, he pictured "neither art nor philosophy, just the perpetual caretaking of the museum of human history."
Here's the thing. The hunger found somewhere to go. The feed. Every day you can be disrespected, and every day you can answer back. It never resolves. That's what keeps you there.

## 5 · The vote
**TED:** There was even a roll call on it. September 19th, 2025. The House passed H. Res. 719, honoring Charlie Kirk's life and legacy. 310 to 58. 38 Democrats voted present.
A resolution like that changes no law and spends no money. It's a vote on recognition — on how a man is remembered. The no votes said they objected to the praise in it, not to condemning the killing.
Same fight Megyn had in the theater. Congress just keeps a tally.

## 6 · Not mappable
**TED:** Now the other movie. Megalopolis, 2024. Francis Ford Coppola put around a hundred and twenty million dollars of his own money into a fable about an architect who can stop time. It lost most of it. And it made people mad.
But here's Nate's point.
**[TAPE — Contain 9:34–10:02, 10:30–10:45]** "…how polarizing the response has been, but how it doesn't really follow that… it pushed buttons, but not in a really sort of mappable way."
**TED:** Not mappable. It made people angry without telling them which camp to stand in. Neither side claimed it. Letterboxd laughed at it. Nate's friends loved it.
Diatribe sixteen on Nate's line. Calm. He isn't fighting anyone — he's describing something he saw. Across the whole episode he's the quietest voice in this special.
Barrett, the host, goes further.
**[TAPE — Contain 42:09–42:50]** "…when we can articulate visions for a better future… that's utopia in and of itself… this was a sort of anti end of history movie."
**TED:** An anti end of history movie. They're aiming straight at Fukuyama.
Fair is fair, though. Contain runs hot too. Later in the same episode Barrett calls the online right non-creators, among other things. Diatribe eighty-two — higher than anything Megyn said. Prism doesn't care which side the heat comes from.

## 7 · The read
**TED:** So here's the read.
Fukuyama said the danger after 1992 wasn't a rival system. It was that people need to be recognized, and a comfortable world doesn't do it for them.
Megyn wants her people recognized. She says so out loud. That's thymos, and it's honest.
Nate and Barrett want recognition too. They just think it comes from making things, not winning the feed. In Barrett's words: people equate recognition with influence, and there's no correlation there anymore.
Two films. Same hunger. One got sorted into a camp in a single scene. One refused to sort, and paid for it at the box office.
The question Prism asks is simple. Did the work get met as a work?
For three hours in a packed theater in Connecticut, it did. For one scene, it didn't.
Maybe the way out of the long 2014 is about that size. Megyn already said it: it's not a political movie, except for this one scene. Go see it.
That's the special. Nate's on Twitter at y2kmindset. I'm Ted Turner. Goodnight from the Getty Villa.
