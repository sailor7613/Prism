## 2 · The long 2014
**TED:** Hello and welcome to the Getty Villa. I'm Ted Turner, and this is a Parallelogram Report special.
No bill on the desk tonight. A documentary, a fraud, and a movie about a man who can stop time.
My friend Nate — Nathan, y2kmindset on Twitter — has a name for the decade we've been living in. He calls it the long 2014.
Here's what he means. Around 2014, the feeds learned something. The fastest way to get your attention is to hand you a side.
So everything new gets run through two camps. A movie. A founder. A guy who posts too much. You're for them or against them. Either way, the feed wins.
The calendar kept moving. The culture didn't. That's the long 2014.

## 3 · A good witch to burn

Two weeks ago, Megyn Kelly told her audience to go see it. She was angry about one scene. This week she came back to it with Mike Solana, who runs Pirate Wires and works at Peter Thiel's Founders Fund. And she went further.
**[TAPE — 0:40–0:50]** "…I think he was intentionally painted the villain by the filmmaker."
**[TAPE — 1:09–1:52]** "…I actually think that he is a caretaker of Elizabeth… some of us women cannot cook… I think Elizabeth had agency in the whole Theranos fraud. I really do."
**TED:** Hold on to that, because it's fair. Billy is the guy the internet loves to laugh at. Megyn reads him as a caretaker. Diatribe score fourteen. That's the calm end of the scale — she isn't fighting anyone, she's describing a man.

**[TAPE — 1:52–2:46]** "…why did Carlos Watson… get a get-out-of-jail-free pass? And Elizabeth Holmes is still sitting her ass in jail…"

## 4 · 1992
**TED:** So why does this keep happening? Why is every founder a genius or a witch, and never just a person who committed a crime and got a sentence?
In 1992 — the year I was born — Francis Fukuyama sat down with Charlie Rose to talk about The End of History and the Last Man. He used an old Greek word.
**[TAPE — 1992, 283–301]** "…it's the part of the personality that demands recognition from other people. It's the source of self-esteem…"
**TED:** Thymos. The part of you that wants to be recognized. Not fed. Not paid. Seen.
In the book he splits it in two. One side wants to be recognized as greater than other people. He calls that megalothymia. The other side wants to be recognized as equal. Isothymia.
His bet was that a liberal society keeps the first one safe by giving it somewhere to go. Start a company. Make a movie. Be the next big founder.
And here's where Charlie Rose read him the critics: with no rival left, we're headed for a world of banality.
**[TAPE — 1992, 502.8–530.0]** "…if people say that, I agree with them perfectly… there's a side of man that really wants great causes… struggle and sacrifice… risk and daring."
**TED:** I agree with them perfectly. That's the man who said history was over.
So put it together. A young founder who wants to be the next Steve Jobs is megalothymia. A crowd that wants her taken back down to size is isothymia. Fukuyama thought a comfortable world might leave both with nowhere to go.
They found somewhere. The feed. Every day there's someone new to raise up, and someone to burn. It never resolves. That's what keeps you there.

## 5 · Not mappable
**TED:** Which brings me to the other movie, and Nate.
Megalopolis, 2024. Francis Ford Coppola put around a hundred and twenty million dollars of his own money into a fable about an architect who can stop time. The title has the same Greek root — megalo, great. It lost most of the money. And it made people furious.
**[TAPE — Contain 9:34–10:02, 10:30–10:45]** "…how polarizing the response has been, but how it doesn't really follow that… it pushed buttons, but not in a really sort of mappable way."
**TED:** Not mappable. It made people angry without telling them which camp to stand in. Neither side claimed it. Nate's friends loved it.
Diatribe sixteen on that line. Nate is the calmest voice in this whole special. He isn't fighting anybody. He's describing something he saw.
Then his host, Barrett, says Mike Solana's sentence — from the other corner.
**[TAPE — Contain 65:45–66:12]** "…if you don't have finance backing you, you have to come with your own story, and you have to be able to articulate it…"
**TED:** Same sentence. Tell your own story, or someone else will. Mike's answer is: be loud online, even crazy if you have to. Barrett's answer is the opposite. Earlier in the episode he says people confuse recognition with influence, and there's no connection there anymore. So make the thing.
**[TAPE — Contain 42:09–42:50]** "…when we can articulate visions for a better future… that's utopia in and of itself… this was a sort of anti end of history movie."
**TED:** An anti end of history movie. They're aiming straight at Fukuyama.
Fair is fair, though. Contain runs hot too. Later in the same episode Barrett calls the online right non-creators, among other things. Diatribe eighty-two — higher than anything Megyn or Mike said. Prism doesn't care which side the heat comes from.

## 6 · The read
**TED:** So here's the read.
Everyone in this special wants the same thing for someone. Megyn wants Billy and Elizabeth seen as people. Mike wants the witch hunt called off. Nate and Barrett want a strange old man's movie seen as a movie.
That's thymos, every time. It's honest.
Prism asks a smaller question. Did the person, or the work, get met on the record?
For Holmes, the record is clear. She defrauded investors. She was acquitted on the patients. She got eleven years. Whether that's in proportion is a real question, and there's a real form for it, sitting at the pardon office. That's where the light gets through.
The witch talk is where it doesn't.
And the funny part: Megyn and Mike both think the film is worth seeing. Mike calls it brilliant. On that, every corner agrees. Go see it. And see Megalopolis while you're at it.
That's the special. Nate's on Twitter at y2kmindset. I'm Ted Turner. Goodnight from the Getty Villa.
