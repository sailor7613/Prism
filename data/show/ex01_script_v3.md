## 3 · The reading
*[screen: needle]* We read five big buyers. Last October, ninety-one percent of the oil they bought was sold in dollars. In August, ninety.

*[screen: needle]* One point. The needle says holding. But in July it hit eighty-seven, the low of the year. Then it came back.

*[M3P low · camera on the globe at [27, 51, 2.2]]* Now look at it from America's side. Last fall, about eight percent of the oil America imported came from the Persian Gulf. In July, zero. Not one barrel.

*[M3P dock · screen: america · camera on the globe at [10, -67, 2.4]]* Our own hemisphere filled the gap, up to ninety-eight percent. Venezuela went from about a hundred and thirty thousand barrels a day to seven hundred thousand. Five times as much.

*[screen: america · camera back to the two-shot]* And every one of those barrels gets paid in dollars. That's how the American market works. So the shock re-routed America's barrel without touching its money.

*[screen: russia · camera on the globe at [38, 62, 3.4]]* Then where did the dollar lose ground? Mostly here. Russia to India, the biggest blue line, paid in rupees, dirhams and yuan. Last fall, about two million barrels a day. By July, almost three million.

*[screen: russia · camera on the globe at [28, 52, 2.8]]* Then August. Russia to India fell back to about two point two million. And the Gulf came back to America. About half a million barrels a day. The weekly numbers say it's still coming.

*[screen: needle · camera back to the two-shot · Ted: the floating head]* So the summer pulled the world's barrel away from the dollar. August pulled it back. That's why we watch it month by month.
