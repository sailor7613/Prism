## 2 · Where we're standing

Two years ago, my friend Nate — y2kmindset on Twitter — went on a podcast called Contain, hosted by Barrett Avner, to talk about Megalopolis. Somewhere in there, Nate named the decade we'd been living in. He called it the long 2014.
**[READ — Nate, Contain 7:38]** "Each cultural artifact that's come out in the last decade, since the year 2014, has been ensconced in this kind of hype cycle — based on riling up polarization among roughly two different, well-defined groups."

## 3 · No memo from CNN
**TED:** When somebody says American democracy is dying, it sounds huge. You picture the sky falling. A formal announcement.
It doesn't work like that. CNN is never going to read out a memo saying democracy just ended.
It goes in moments. Moments where the contradiction is right out in the open. Iran-Contra was one. The government sold weapons to Iran to fund a war Congress had said no to. The country found out, and things went on.
This midterm is another one of those moments. Maybe the plainest yet.
Here's why. For the whole long 2014, we kept looking for history in a candidate. Obama and hope. Then Bernie. Then Trump. Left or right, somebody was going to smash the norms and open the whole thing up.
I was one of those people. I used to think Bernie or Elizabeth Warren would have changed everything. Now I think they'd have been another round of Obama. A program blocked at home, while Russia and China kept moving. Before 2022, Russia supplied around forty percent of the gas Europe imported. That's where history was happening. Not in our primaries.
So here's this week. Same war, same gas prices. Gas was two ninety-eight before the war with Iran. It's four twenty-nine now. Diesel is over six dollars — a record.
The president, in Perry, Georgia.
**[TAPE — Trump, Perry GA, 2026-10-24 · trump_01_fortune 80.0–117.0]** "…I could just see the fake headlines… people are suffering at the pump… But I said, I don't give a damn. I'm going to say it. We're making a freaking fortune… and those pump prices are going to come down."
**TED:** Barack Obama, at a rally in Georgia the same week.
**[TAPE — Obama, United for Georgia rally · obama_01_iran 64.0–107.0]** "…Iran now has veto power over the Strait of Hormuz… This is the reason why the price of oil is up… this war is not over. It's still going on."

## 4 · What now
**TED:** So if the memo's never coming, the question is: what now? What kind of talk is still possible?
For the last ten years, the smart answer has been a kind of lazy optimism. Let the internet do its thing. It's mostly a swamp. Porn, racism, garbage. But something interesting will come out of it on its own.
And some things did. Some of it was beautiful. But I think we've used that up.
Here's what happens when you have a lot of contradictions and no bigger game to play. Edmund Berger has a name for it — reciprocal contradictions. Two sides that need each other's failures. When that's all there is, the only way out is irony. People backing whoever makes the joke land.
I'll be honest. It's hard to make a positive case for anyone on the ballot this year with a straight face. Not on either side. That's not a reason to stop paying attention. It's a reason to want something better to pay attention to.

## 5 · Two years on
**TED:** Which is where Megalopolis comes in.
Two years ago, Francis Ford Coppola put around a hundred and twenty million dollars of his own money into a fable about an architect who can stop time. It flopped. People hated it.
Barrett had a line about who hated it.
**[READ — Barrett, Contain 12:12]** "Why is it that everybody I like personally, who I think is smart, really likes this movie? And then people who wear Plantasia tote bags, or pin their identity to some kind of political movement of the past eight years — just cannot understand it at all."
**TED:** People who still put their hope in the last ten years of politics. They couldn't place it. And Nate saw why.
**[READ — Nate, Contain 9:34]** "What's interesting is how polarizing the response has been — but it doesn't really follow the typical polarization on political lines."
**TED:** It made people angry without telling them which camp to stand in.
Digger, this fall, is the opposite. Alejandro Iñárritu and Tom Cruise. An oil man knew a giant ice shelf was going to hit Europe, and he let it happen. In its big scene, he stands in front of the European Parliament and says it out loud: you needed the energy, I sold it to you.
That's the confession we keep waiting for. Then the movie looks away. It tells you it was all a play. Then it walks outside, and the world is a wasteland. The bill gets paid by the weather.
Here's how Prism sees that. We measure something called lumens. Lumens is how much of the actual thing gets through when someone talks. And it depends on which way you're facing. Turn toward the thing, and you catch light. Turn toward the argument about the argument, and you're in shadow, from any corner.
Digger faces the thing, then turns away. Megalopolis faces it and walks toward it. It doesn't confess. It proposes. A city. A future you can argue with.

## 6 · Far from the club
**TED:** That's what this show is trying to be. Prism started as an aesthetic project as much as anything. Lumens is a sense of when talk is beautiful because it's true. We're not here to tell you who to vote for. We're here to turn toward the thing.
There's a scene in Megalopolis everybody laughs at. Barrett reads it better than anyone.
**[READ — Barrett, Contain 56:26]** "It's that scene in the club where he's spinning out of control, losing his mind. That's the one part of the movie where he loses his ability to stop time — because he's indulging in society shit."
**TED:** So get as far from the club as you can. Face the thing. Gather enough light that you can give some back.
