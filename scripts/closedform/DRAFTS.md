- **Draft 3.** Rewording alone did not tighten the band. Reading each reader's reasons showed why: the pattern library steers Qwen, the question showed today's Object to old lines, and a three-way choice gives Gemma a middle to retreat to.

It worked. The verdict question holds (83% · 86%); the reason question is now the loose one (67% · 58%). Each reader's own spread on Tucker fell to about 3 points.   *Not fixed:* the Dia number itself. The three land within 5 points of each other on 17% of Tucker's lines and 36% of the Crystal's.   DeepSeek, tracked: its own spread is down to 13 and 10 points (from 38 and 34 at draft 1); it matches the majority's band on 68% and 77% of lines.

## The deadlock rule, 2026-11-17

Ruled by Sailor while setting the Object for the Jensen set. It is a standing rule of method, not part of any one draft.

When setting a sign would mean choosing which of two opposed subjects is realized, the sign is one level too low. Go up one level, to what both say they are serving, and set it there. What they disagree about becomes a means: recorded by date, never scored. The test is that both sides would accept the wording of the sign. Sailor's phrase: to open a new level of syntax.

First use: the afternoon ruling "restricted chip sales is realized" was withdrawn the same day. The Jensen set is read against America's standing against China in AI. The chip rule is the means. Nothing is measured of that standing yet, so the set has no Lumens.

## Rulings and workflow, 2026-11-18 (from the walk)

Sailor ruled three things on the Jensen set, and changed how drafts are run. All four are in `rulings.json`.

1. **The Object spoken out loud takes no side.** "We want the United States to be ahead" reads neither. It states the shared goal, which is the level the sign now sits at.
2. **Dual realization.** Two opposed subjects can both read realized at once. It is a state of the Object, not a fault. The conversion is read from the lines, from what each side concedes. To build: a question, "what does this line give up to the other side?"
3. **A concession on the rival's gain reads realized.** "If China were to discover a cure for cancer, it's great for us."
4. **Workflow.** Each draft runs on Qwen alone. The full grid runs at checkpoints. The local reader gets a fixed brief.

The Jensen set is no longer unseen: rulings 1 and 3 quote its lines. From here, agreement on it shows the rules were learned, as with the working set. The next unseen set is whatever is filed next.

## Draft 6 — the rulings in the questions (prediction written 2026-11-20, before the run)

**What changed** (backups of both files: `archive/draft6_2026-11-20_before/`):
- **The side question** (grammar, key g6). A question, a fact with no because, a comparison, a value or a feeling: false. A prayer that shuts a side out or calls their position a spell is a verdict; one that names no one is not.
