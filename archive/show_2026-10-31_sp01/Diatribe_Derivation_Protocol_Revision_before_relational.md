## 1.4.0 — What It Measures

The quality of engagement with the political object. Receives X, Y, and Z as inputs. Receives displacement context from Module 9. Produces the encoding's most consequential score.

**Scale:** 0–100

- 0–33: Fluid. The fluid coordinate subject is dominant. Can see the object. Can be surprised. Can hold ambivalence.
- 34–66: Oscillation zone. The subject is in transit between the fluid and denominated coordinate subjects. Capacity for surprise partially operative. Substance still accessible but requires effort. Recovery possible.
- 67–100: Denominated dominant. The denominated coordinate subject has captured the oscillation. Cannot be surprised. Object destroyed and replaced.

The Diatribe measures where in the oscillation between the two coordinate subjects the discourse sits. At the fluid pole, the subject engages the Object directly. At the denominated pole, the subject responds to the tribal signal the Object has been denominated into. The middle is not a third position — it is the transit between two positions that each have independent epistemic standing.

## 1.4.1 — Displacement / Denomination Separation

### Definitions

**Displacement** is the structural pressure the Object exerts on the coordinate subject at a given quadrant. It is determined by three factors: the Object itself (what is being contested), Object Z (realized/frustrated), and the prevalent axis (which axis carries the primary split). Displacement is a function of position — it is set by coordinates, not by the quality of the subject's engagement.

**Denomination** is whether the coordinate subject buckled under displacement pressure or held. It is the Diatribe measurement: the quality of engagement with the political object. Denomination is a function of response — it measures what the subject did with the pressure it received.

### Independence

Displacement and denomination are correlated — high displacement creates pressure toward denomination — but they are independent measurements. They come apart in both directions:

**High displacement, low denomination:** The coordinate subject is under maximum structural pressure but still engages the Object. Capacity for surprise intact. This is the Didion position — C at maximum displacement across forty years of the immigration arc, Diatribe 10. The displacement is enormous; the engagement is genuine. This combination is what fluidity looks like under duress.

**High displacement, high denomination:** The coordinate subject is under maximum structural pressure AND has succumbed to denomination. The intensity of displacement fuels the denomination — the harder the Object pushes, the more tempting the tribal vehicle becomes. The displacement is real; the engagement is denominated.

**Low displacement, high denomination:** The coordinate subject is under minimal structural pressure but denominates anyway. The Object barely touches this quadrant, yet the subject replaces it with a tribal signal. This is diagnostic of pre-existing denomination — the subject's X-perception was captured before the Object arrived. The displacement matrix predicts low pressure; the Diatribe measures high denomination. The delta between expected and actual denomination is a signal of prior capture.

**Low displacement, low denomination:** Minimal pressure, genuine engagement. Default state. Least diagnostically interesting.

### Compounding

Displacement and denomination can compound in either direction. High displacement can fuel denomination (the pressure makes the vehicle more tempting) or fuel deeper fluid engagement (the pressure makes the stakes real enough to hold). The direction of compounding is itself diagnostic — it reveals whether the coordinate subject's relationship to the modes of political economy is strong enough to withstand the Object's pressure.

### Implication for Scoring

A Diatribe score only means something in context of the displacement pressure that produced it. Diatribe 45 under low displacement is a different finding than Diatribe 45 under maximum displacement. The first suggests the subject chose denomination despite minimal pressure (prior capture). The second suggests the subject is in the oscillation zone under genuine duress (coalition state — still recoverable). Step 10 of the derivation protocol formalizes this interpretation.

## 1.4.2 — Derivation Procedure (Ten Steps)

### Workflow note

The Diatribe derivation protocol is the final step in the encoding sequence. It runs after Module 9 has derived the coordinate subjects and their positions. The order matters: derive positions first (Module 9), then score the discourse (Module 1.4). This ensures the Diatribe score is calibrated against positions that have already been independently derived, not against positions the Diatribe itself determined.

---

### Step 1 — Establish the political object

What is actually at stake? Not the framing, not the rhetoric, not the tribal signal — the underlying substance. If the object cannot be identified, that is itself diagnostic: the object may already have been destroyed by denomination.

State the Object in one sentence. This sentence must pass Module 5 validation — it describes what is being contested, not how it is being discussed.

---

### Step 2 — Receive displacement context

Before scoring, the protocol must know how much structural pressure each quadrant is under. This step receives three inputs from Module 9:

**2a — Prevalent axis.** Which axis carries the primary split for this event? X-prevalent events split left/right. Y-prevalent events split institutional/grassroots. This determines which coordinate subjects are across the primary divide from each other and which share a side.

**2b — Object Z.** The Object's promise-delivery gap, scored per Module 1.3. High Object Z (realized, +0.60 to +0.95) means the apparatus delivered. Low Object Z (frustrated, -0.60 to -0.95) means it failed. Near-zero means the gap is ambiguous.

**2c — Displacement matrix position.** For each quadrant, determine its position in the displacement matrix:

| Relative to Object | Prevalent axis | Non-prevalent axis | Displacement level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Same / Same | Aligned on primary split | Shared mechanism | Minimum |
| Same / Opposite | Aligned on primary split | Opposing mechanism | Low-moderate |
| Opposite / Same | Across primary divide | Shared mechanism | Moderate-high |
| Opposite / Opposite | Across primary divide | Opposing mechanism | Maximum |

Object Z scales the matrix. High Object Z intensifies displacement for opposing-prevalent-axis positions. Low Object Z creates pressure on +Y positions to account for institutional failure.

**Output:** Four displacement levels (one per quadrant) plus prevalent axis and Object Z. These are context for scoring, not scores themselves.

---

### Step 3 — Assess coordinate subject dominance

For each quadrant being scored, determine which coordinate subject is dominant in the discourse:

**Fluid coordinate subject dominant:** The subject engages the Object directly. X-perception operative, capacity for surprise intact. The discourse sounds like what Module 9 Step 4 derived — the logically necessary fluid response at these coordinates.

**Denominated coordinate subject dominant:** The subject responds to the tribal signal. X-perception captured, capacity for surprise lost. The discourse sounds like what Module 9 Step 5 derived — the logically necessary denominated response at these coordinates.

**Oscillation:** The subject is in transit. It can see the Object but feels the pull of the denomination vehicle forming on its side. The discourse oscillates between the two derived positions — substance is still present but tribal coloring is visible.

This is the protocol's most fundamental diagnostic. The binary from the prior version (YES/NO on capacity for surprise) is retained as a quick gate:

- **YES** → Fluid coordinate subject dominant or oscillation zone. Proceed to Step 4 for gradient placement.
- **NO** → Denominated coordinate subject dominant. Diatribe 67+. Proceed to Step 4 to determine how far into denomination the subject has traveled.

---

### Step 4 — Apply the denomination gradient

Place the discourse on the five-stage denomination gradient (Module 3 / 🔥 D). The prior version used the three-level conspiratorial gradient as the primary scale. The revision generalizes: the conspiratorial gradient is one instance of the denomination grammar, not the whole thing.

**Stage 0 — Liquid (Diatribe 0–10).** The subject encounters the Object without pre-formed interpretive framework. Maximum capacity for surprise. No denomination vehicle present. Pure fluidity. Rare in the scoring library — most subjects arrive with some orientation.

**Stage 1 — Vehicle emergence (Diatribe 10–33).** The subject has an orientation toward the Object, but the orientation remains falsifiable. Proportionate empirical engagement. Observations are proportionate and revisable. The interpretive frame serves the subject's encounter with the Object rather than replacing it.

**Stage 2 — Frame consolidation (Diatribe 34–55).** The orientation has solidified into a frame. The frame begins to filter the Object rather than serve it. Disproportionate emphasis without totalization — the subject can still engage contrary evidence but defaults to frame-consistent interpretation. The denomination vehicle is forming but has not replaced the substance. This is the oscillation zone: the subject can see what denomination looks like on its side and is trying not to do it.

**Stage 3 — Substance replacement (Diatribe 55–80).** The frame has replaced the substance. The subject responds to its narrative about the Object rather than the Object itself. Heroes and villains have replaced mechanisms and tradeoffs. The capacity for surprise is severely degraded — the subject can be surprised in theory but in practice absorbs new information into the frame. Most scored denomination patterns in the library operate at Stage 3.

**Stage 4 — Totalization (Diatribe 80–100).** The denomination vehicle has expanded from domain-specific to comprehensive. The subject's entire interpretive framework is the vehicle. New objects are immediately absorbed rather than encountered. Capacity for surprise is destroyed. Equivalent to 📓d Level 3 conspiratorial detachment and 📓b theological denomination Level 3. Both converge here by different routes — total denomination, zero liquidity.

**Mapping to prior version's conspiratorial gradient:**

| Prior version | Revised version |
|---|---|
| Level 1 (Dia 5–20): Proportionate | Stage 0–1: Liquid / Vehicle emergence |
| Level 2 (Dia 25–45): Disproportionate | Stage 2: Frame consolidation |
| Level 3 (Dia 60–80): Conspiratorial | Stage 3–4: Substance replacement / Totalization |

The five-stage gradient applies to all destruction modes, not only the conspiratorial one. Performative denomination follows the same stages through institutional/discursive vehicles. Defensive denomination follows them through non-engagement and silence. The conspiratorial gradient remains valid as a description of how conspiratorial denomination specifically moves through the stages — it is a subset, not the general case.

---

### Step 5 — Identify the destruction mode (if Diatribe > 33)

When the denomination gradient places the discourse at Stage 2 or above, identify which destruction mode is operative. The five modes and their pattern counts:

| Mode | Mechanism | Pattern count | Detection signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reactionary | Negation of the other's subjectivity | 1 pattern (#17) | Ask "which vehicle?" not "which pattern?" One operation, many carriers. |
| Performative | Institutional/discursive performance substituting for engagement | 14 patterns | Split between stated position and institutional action. Most frequent mode. |
| Defensive | Non-engagement, definitional retreat, silence as strategy | 7 patterns | Full lifecycle: single (#4) → compounding (#15) → perceptual blindness (#24) → terminal erasure (#26) |
| Conspiratorial | Reclassification of disagreement as evidence of hidden monstrous allegiance | 2 patterns (#5, #12) | Operates identically on both X-axis sides. |
| Denial | Left-side conspiratorial variant — denies real phenomena exist rather than totalizing them | 2 patterns (#8, #13) | Same structural effect as conspiratorial: object destroyed. |

**Shared dependency with Module 9.** The 26 patterns in the denomination library now serve dual roles. In Module 9 (response generation), the patterns are what the denominated coordinate subject *is derived from* — they are the generative ground for the denominated position. In Module 1.4 (this protocol), the patterns are what the Diatribe *detects* — they are the diagnostic tools for identifying how denomination happened. Same library, different reads. Not circular: the patterns have independent epistemic standing from 64 scored entries across five domains.

When identifying the operative mode, reference the specific patterns by number and name. Multiple modes can be simultaneously operative for a single event — report all that apply with the dominant mode flagged.

---

### Step 6 — Check for spread vs. composite

When multiple actors, institutions, or responses within a single event span 30+ Diatribe points, report the spread alongside the composite. The spread reveals the event's Diatribe structure.

Rule: Composite obscures structure when spread > 30. Always report both.

Calibration reference: 📓e4 Event 7 — children Diatribe 0, grassroots 10–15, Miller 85–90. Composite 65 obscures the structure entirely.

---

### Step 7 — Dual-destruction detection (Soros encoding problem)

Can the same object produce radically different Diatribe scores depending on how it is invoked? If yes, score the *invocation*, not the object.

Generalized diagnostic: Does the axis name a specific person as the agent of systemic phenomena? If yes → reject axis (Category 1), reroute to mechanism-framed axis. Any figure simultaneously legitimately criticizable and conspiratorially totalized will become defensively protected, and the object is destroyed twice — by totalization and by denial.

---

### Step 8 — Apply symmetry test

Structurally identical mechanisms must score within 5-point range regardless of X-axis side. Cross-session actor consistency required. If the encoding produces asymmetric scores for symmetric mechanisms, the encoding is biased — recalibrate.

Validated symmetry pairs (all within 5 points):
- Clinton-Gabbard (#5, Dia 75–80) ≈ Pizzagate (#12, Dia 75–80) ≈ 51 officials (#11, Dia 75–80)
- Progressive non-engagement on immigration (#15, Dia 55–70) ≈ Conservative non-engagement on healthcare (#15, Dia 55–70)
- Rubio disavowal of Gang of Eight (📓e4, Dia 55–60) ≈ Romney disavowal of own healthcare plan (📓e3, Dia 55)

---

### Step 9 — Assign oscillation tags

Tag the score by its position in the coordinate-subject oscillation:

**Diatribe 0–33 — Fluid dominant.** The fluid coordinate subject holds the oscillation. The denominated coordinate subject exists logically but does not dominate the discourse. No destruction mode operative. The subject engages the Object from its coordinates.

**Diatribe 34–66 — Oscillation zone.** The subject is in transit between the two coordinate subjects. Tag with trajectory indicator: is the oscillation migrating toward denomination (progressive denomination) or toward fluidity (denomination recovery)? The direction is more diagnostically important than the static score. A Diatribe of 50 trending toward 40 is a fundamentally different finding than a Diatribe of 50 trending toward 60.

**Diatribe 67–100 — Denominated dominant.** The denominated coordinate subject has captured the oscillation. The destruction mode identified in Step 5 is operative — this is what denomination looks like at these coordinates. The fluid coordinate subject still exists logically (it can be derived) but has been displaced from the discourse.

**Anti-valent designation.** One of the three oscillation positions (fluid dominant, oscillation zone, denominated dominant) may be anti-valent for a given event — the position that lacks empirical traction. If the discourse is overwhelmingly denominated, fluidity may be anti-valent (the position exists logically but has almost no real occupants). If the discourse is still liquid, denomination may be anti-valent. Designate with a one-line rationale.

---

### Step 10 — Displacement-calibrated interpretation

The final step asks: what does this score mean given the displacement pressure this quadrant is under?

Read the Diatribe score against the displacement level assigned in Step 2. The displacement level sets the *expected* denomination range — what the structural position alone would predict. The Diatribe score measures the *actual* denomination. The relationship between expected and actual is the interpretive finding.

**Expected alignment (displacement predicts denomination):**

- High displacement → high denomination: The structural pressure is producing the denomination the matrix predicts. The system is working as the displacement model expects. Diagnostically routine — the pressure pushed, the subject buckled. The question is which mode and which patterns, not whether the denomination is surprising.

- Low displacement → low denomination: Minimal pressure, genuine engagement. The system is at rest. Least interesting diagnostic state.

**Deviation (displacement and denomination come apart):**

- High displacement → low denomination: The coordinate subject is holding under maximum pressure. This is the Didion finding — and it is the encoding's most important diagnostic signal. It identifies the positions in the discourse that are still doing dialectical work under conditions where denomination would be structurally predicted. In arc mode, a quadrant that sustains high displacement and low denomination across multiple events has proven its relationship to the modes of political economy is strong enough to withstand the Object's pressure. Mark as **resilient fluidity under duress.**

- Low displacement → high denomination: The coordinate subject denominates despite minimal structural pressure. The Object barely touches this quadrant, but the subject has already replaced it with a tribal signal. This identifies pre-existing capture — the subject's X-perception was compromised before the Object arrived. In arc mode, a quadrant that shows high denomination under low displacement across multiple events reveals that the denomination is not being produced by this Object but by a prior history of denomination that the current Object merely activates. Mark as **prior capture — displacement-independent denomination.**

**Output per quadrant:** Diatribe score, denomination stage, destruction mode(s) if applicable, oscillation tag with trajectory, displacement level, and displacement-denomination alignment or deviation with interpretive note.

## 1.4.3 — Axis Interaction Rules (Revised)

The original five rules are retained as findings. Each is now grounded in the displacement matrix mechanism, giving them predictive rather than merely descriptive force.

### Rule 1 — Y-prevalent events produce lower Diatribe than X-prevalent events

**Mechanism:** When the prevalent axis is Y, the primary split runs institutional/grassroots rather than left/right. Coordinate subjects who would normally oppose each other on X find themselves on the same side of the primary divide. To get there, each had to override its X-perception and engage the Object on Y terms. That override requires capacity for surprise to be fully operative — the subject must be able to say "my usual X-axis allies are wrong on this one." This is a self-selecting filter for fluidity.

The displacement matrix explains it: Y-prevalent events route around the X-axis tribal machinery. X is where denomination infrastructure lives in American discourse. Y-splitting runs on a less-denominated axis.

**Predictive application:** When scoring a Y-prevalent event, expect lower Diatribe scores across all quadrants. When Diatribe is unexpectedly high on a Y-prevalent event, look for X-axis capture — the tribal machinery is colonizing a split that should be running on Y.

### Rule 2 — Form-substance divergence is a displacement modifier

**Mechanism:** When an actor operates through +Y institutional channels while performing -Y rhetoric (or vice versa), the displacement matrix assigns them to one cell based on form and a different cell based on substance. The coordinate subject at that quadrant must process a contradictory signal. This contradiction creates additional Diatribe pressure beyond what the displacement matrix alone predicts.

The divergence itself is measurable — Y-form score and Y-substance score, separated by slash notation (per Module 1.2). The gap between them is a Diatribe input: maintaining a form-substance divergence requires denomination, because the subject must either not notice the contradiction (denomination) or actively perform it (Pattern #2 — performative institutional denomination).

### Rule 3 — Large Z-gaps create Diatribe pressure

**Mechanism:** Object Z determines displacement magnitude. A large Z-gap creates displacement pressure on every coordinate subject, but asymmetrically. The +Y coordinate subjects inside the realizing apparatus face a specific choice: confront the gap (acknowledge that the institution delivered something the discourse claims it didn't) or migrate toward the denominated coordinate subject (make the gap invisible). The Z-gap is displacement pressure aimed specifically at the oscillation between the two coordinate subjects. The bigger the gap, the harder it is to stay at the fluid pole.

Z-gaps function as Diatribe *pressure* rather than Diatribe *scores*. A Z-gap of 0.80 doesn't automatically produce a Diatribe of 80. It produces displacement pressure that pushes the oscillation toward denomination. Where the oscillation settles — the Diatribe score — depends on whether the coordinate subject holds or buckles.

### Rule 4 — Designed Z-gaps produce maximum Diatribe pressure

**Mechanism:** Designed Z-gaps (Pattern #16) produce permanent, non-resolvable displacement pressure. The gap cannot be closed because the mechanism *is* the gap — it structurally produces the problem it claims to address. The oscillation between fluid and denominated coordinate subjects is under constant pressure. The fluid coordinate subject at +Y must either abandon the institution (which requires leaving its Y-coordinate) or migrate to denomination (which makes the design invisible). The only exit from permanent displacement is changing coordinates.

The Iran-Contra arc exemplifies this at scale: the imperial cycle (foreign intervention → displacement → domestic political fuel → further intervention) is an architectural Z-gap sustained across forty years. Object Z at +0.95 for four decades. The displacement pressure on C never lets up — and C never buckles. That sustained resilience is a forty-year Didion finding.

### Rule 5 — Migratory Diatribe is the oscillation moving over time

**Mechanism:** In the two-coordinate-subject model, migratory Diatribe is the oscillation migrating over time. The coordinate subject doesn't change position — it's still at the same quadrant coordinates. What changes is where in the oscillation between fluidity and denomination the subject sits.

Longitudinal tracking captures this movement: is the subject migrating toward the denominated pole (progressive denomination) or toward the fluid pole (denomination recovery)?

Arc mode in the Parallelogram makes this visible. The Diatribe trajectory across an arc's events — plotted on the graphmap timeline scrub — shows the oscillation's migration for each quadrant. The direction and rate of migration are more diagnostically important than any single score.

Calibration references:
- Rubio: 25 → 60 → 80 (progressive denomination across arc, acceleration at each step)
- Captive dreamer: 60 → 65 → 70 (progressive denomination, slower acceleration — displacement is constant but the subject is still fighting)
- C across CS04 immigration arc: 10 → 10 → 10 (resilient fluidity under sustained maximum displacement — the Didion pattern)
